# RestaBee homepage SEO and content brief

Prepared: 25 October 2026.
Scope: keyword targeting, search intent, content structure, proposed copy, internal linking, metadata, evidence requirements and SEO measurement for `https://restabee.com/`.

This standalone brief is based on the supplied website SEO strategy, keyword page map, landing-page feature brief and public pricing document. It does not use the existing website's design or copy as a requirement. It contains no visual design direction, styling specification or instruction to preserve a layout. Proposed content is subject to product and commercial review; this document does not describe implementation status.

## 1. Homepage objective

Help an India-based restaurant operator understand RestaBee's restaurant POS offering, evaluate the order-to-bill workflow and request a demo.

Primary audience: independent restaurants, cafés, QSRs, takeaway businesses and small growing multi-outlet teams. These are target audiences, not claims about existing customers.

Primary business outcome: a qualified demo enquiry. Supporting outcomes: understanding the product, evaluating pricing and discovering relevant feature or solution pages.

The page should answer:

1. What is RestaBee, and which restaurant workflows does it support?
2. How does an order move from staff entry to kitchen preparation and billing?
3. Which capabilities matter to my restaurant?
4. Can I manage menus and access across outlets?
5. What does the subscription cost?
6. What should I expect from a demonstration?

## 2. Positioning and message hierarchy

The source documents recommend leading with daily restaurant operations:

**Order → kitchen → service → bill.**

Secondary differentiator: controlled central menu publication and application to selected outlets, alongside independent outlet menus when needed.

Supporting topics: table/party management, item choices, split eligible draft bills, recorded collections, dues, cash shifts, reporting and role/outlet permissions.

Keep internal subscription administration, platform finance, support administration and sales tooling out of the restaurant-facing product story.

The homepage is a product overview and conversion page. It should demonstrate a coherent workflow rather than reproduce every feature description or become a generic definition article.

## 7. Recommended content sequence

This is a narrative and information hierarchy, not a layout or styling specification.

### Section 1 — Product introduction

Use the proposed opening copy above. Establish restaurant POS, audience fit, the order-to-bill mechanism and the demo action immediately.

Evidence: an actual product screenshot or approved demonstration. If a synthetic visual is used, label the interface and values as illustrative. Do not substitute invented customer counts or ratings for proof.

### Section 2 — Service types

Explain these accurately:

- **Dine-in:** capture orders in the restaurant's table/service workflow.
- **Takeaway:** staff-entered counter orders and kitchen handoff.
- **Delivery:** staff-entered delivery orders with applicable charges.

Delivery order entry must not be described as delivery-platform integration, courier dispatch or live tracking.

### Section 3 — Order-to-bill workflow

Proposed H2: **From order to bill, one clear workflow.**

Proposed introduction: **Follow an order from staff entry to the kitchen, through preparation and service, and on to billing and collection.**

1. **Take the order.** Choose the service type and capture item variants, modifiers and kitchen instructions.
2. **Send to the kitchen.** Route items to the appropriate preparation stations.
3. **Track preparation and service.** Follow preparation, mark full or partial quantities ready and coordinate pickup and serving.
4. **Prepare the bill and record collection.** Preview the bill, arrange eligible unissued checks and record how payment was collected.

Use one consistent restaurant example. Keep ready, served and paid distinct. Do not imply zero latency or unrestricted edits after dispatch or billing.

### Section 4 — Main capability overview

Proposed H2: **Restaurant tools for every handoff in service.**

Proposed introduction: **Give the floor, kitchen and cashier the information they need to move each order forward.**

The feature brief proposes eight main capability summaries:

| Capability | Proposed homepage copy | Evidence to obtain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orders for every service type | Take dine-in, takeaway and delivery orders. Add variants, extras and kitchen instructions before sending items for preparation. | Service-type selection, cart and customization dialog |
| A clear view of the floor | See occupied tables, seat waiting parties, assign service staff and move a party when plans change. | Floor and party panel |
| Kitchen tickets by station | Give each kitchen station its preparation queue. Start items, mark quantities ready and track what is ready for pickup. | Station queue and preparation states |
| Flexible restaurant billing | Preview bills, apply permitted discounts and record cash, UPI or card collections, including mixed methods. | Bill preview and collection with two tenders |
| Split bills for the way guests pay | Divide eligible items into separate draft bills or combine eligible orders before issuing a bill. | Draft-check item allocation |
| One menu across outlets | Build a central menu, review changes and apply published updates to selected outlets. Keep outlet menus independent when needed. | Publication review and outlet application |
| Cash shifts and outstanding dues | Track opening cash, drawer movements, shift counts and balances still owed by customers. | Shift summary and due register |
| Reports for restaurant decisions | Review issued sales, confirmed collections, item sales, tax, discounts and cash-shift variance by permitted outlet and date. | Actual report and date/outlet filters |

These are content units, not a requirement for eight identically styled cards. Grouping and presentation should be decided separately from SEO.

### Section 5 — Kitchen and floor proof

Proposed H2: **Keep the floor and kitchen working from current order information.**

Show a real order handoff, station instructions, partial readiness and the service context. Explain what happens when the kitchen marks an item ready. Use precise captions identifying the screen and workflow.

If describing operational updates, demonstrate a change on another terminal. Do not extend that evidence into a promise that every report/settings screen is realtime or background alerts always arrive.

### Section 6 — Central menu and outlet control

Proposed H2: **Prepare menu changes centrally. Publish when ready.**

Proposed copy: **Review changes before publication, then apply published menu updates to selected outlets. Maintain independent outlet menus when needed, with access scoped to the outlets each team member handles.**

Useful example: a menu owner prepares a price or modifier change, reviews it, publishes it and selects outlets for application. Explain controlled publication rather than suggesting silent replacement of every outlet's active orders or prices.

### Section 7 — Billing, collections and cash

Proposed H2: **Prepare the bill. Record how it was settled.**

Cover eligible split/combined draft bills, permitted discounts, tax lines, mixed collections, customer dues and cash shifts. Keep these distinctions explicit:

- Split bills allocate eligible unissued items; split payments record several collection methods.
- Issued sales and confirmed collections are different measures.
- Payment-method recording is not automatic bank verification or settlement.
- Approved bill adjustment and money returned are separate steps.

Link to dedicated billing, split-billing, GST/tax or cash-management pages when published.

### Section 8 — Team access and reporting

Proposed H2: **The right workspace for each team member.**

Proposed copy: **Give each team member access to the work they handle. Review restaurant reports by permitted outlet and date, with records that distinguish sales, collections and cash-shift variance.**

Demonstrate a restaurant role or report example. Do not promise workforce scheduling, payroll, full accounting, profit or food-cost reporting without separate evidence.

### Section 9 — Pricing and evaluation

Proposed H2: **Simple pricing. Complete access.**

Proposed introduction: **Choose a subscription for one restaurant or a growing multi-outlet team, with access to released standard POS features.**

The user selected the public pricing document over the older strategy's tiered pricing proposal:

| Subscription | Total payable | Commercial boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single outlet, 3 months | ₹2,499 | One outlet |
| Single outlet, 6 months | ₹4,299 | One outlet |
| Single outlet, 12 months | ₹6,999 | One outlet |
| Multi-outlet, 12 months | ₹5,999 per outlet | Annual billing; minimum two outlets |

Example: two outlets cost ₹11,998 per year.

Required qualifier: **Prices shown are complete payable subscription amounts, with no GST added separately. Hardware, third-party services and custom work are quoted separately. Planned modules are not yet released.**

Do not publish Essentials/Operations feature tiers, private discount rules or internal financial assumptions. If showing monthly equivalents, label them as equivalents, not monthly billing. Reconfirm public terms and tax wording if the business's circumstances change.

### Section 10 — Buyer FAQs

Answers should resolve purchase objections and clarify operational boundaries. Suggested questions and writing baseline:

**Can I manage dine-in, takeaway and delivery?**

Staff can create orders for each service type and manage their kitchen and billing workflow. Delivery orders are entered by staff; this does not imply automatic imports from delivery platforms.

**Can guests split a bill or use several payment methods?**

Eligible draft bills can be arranged before issue. Collections can be recorded across multiple payment methods. Split bills and split payments are separate controls.

**Can I manage menus for more than one outlet?**

Use reviewed central menu publication and selected-outlet application, or keep outlet menus independent. Access depends on the team member's permitted outlets.

**Can I record delivery and packaging charges?**

Set flat defaults and edit permitted charges per order. Refund handling follows the configured policy and permissions.

**Does recording UPI or card payments mean automatic verification?**

No. Recording collections does not imply bank verification, payment-terminal integration or automatic settlement.

**Which devices and printers can I use?**

Arrange a demo on your intended devices and confirm printer compatibility during setup. Printing requires compatible hardware and the appropriate local setup.

**How do I get started?**

Request a demo and share your restaurant name, city, service types and outlet count. Discuss your menu, devices and printing needs during evaluation.

Do not add guaranteed setup times, support response times, refund promises or app-store/device badges without approved evidence and terms.

### Section 11 — Demo request and contact

Proposed H2: **See RestaBee with your restaurant's workflow.**

Proposed copy: **Tell us about your restaurant, service types and outlets. Walk through an order, kitchen preparation and billing, and discuss the setup your team needs.**

Capture only information the sales process needs: name, restaurant name, city, outlet count, callback contact and an optional message. Explain how contact details will be used. Do not preselect unrelated marketing consent.

A completed demo enquiry must reach the existing CRM. An email link should say it opens email; a form must not display submission success until the server accepts the lead. Do not treat CTA clicks as completed enquiries.

Publish verified business contact information and real policy destinations. Do not add placeholder legal links or invent addresses, social profiles or communication channels.

## 8. Product claim boundaries

| Topic | Permitted subject to product review | Do not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery | Staff-entered delivery orders and flat applicable charges | Aggregator ingestion, dispatch or tracking |
| Payments | Recorded cash, UPI, card and mixed collections | Automatic verification, gateway/EDC integration or settlement |
| GST/tax | Configured profiles and captured bill tax lines | Filing, government certification or e-invoicing |
| Menu availability | Stop/resume sale and reviewed publication | Ingredient inventory or recipe stock deduction |
| Staff | Roles, permissions and outlet-scoped access | Attendance, scheduling or payroll |
| Printing | Bill/KOT documents with compatible local setup | All-printer compatibility, cloud printing or automated WhatsApp receipts |
| Drafts | Draft retention where demonstrated | Offline-authoritative ordering or billing |
| Live operations | Specific operational updates shown in a real demonstration | Universal instant updates or guaranteed background alerts |
| Customer records | Guest/contact details and linked history | Loyalty or automated marketing campaigns |
| Refunds | Adjustment review and money-return records | Automatic execution of provider refunds |

Never invent testimonials, ratings, outlet counts, savings, sales increases, uptime or integration partnerships. A demo screen must not be presented as customer proof. Recheck the dated product capability audit before public release; source inspection is not the same as production acceptance.

## 9. Content evidence and asset requirements

This section specifies what evidence the content needs, not its visual styling.

- Use one consistent synthetic demo restaurant across order, kitchen, floor, menu and billing examples.
- Prefer actual product captures with readable, accurate captions.
- Mask guest phone numbers, account emails and sensitive records.
- Label synthetic values and illustrative screens explicitly.
- Show concrete actions: saving a draft versus sending an order; marking items ready versus serving; issuing a bill versus recording collection.
- If using video, supply a text explanation or transcript so the product story is available without playback.
- Write alt text about the actual task or screenshot; keep decorative imagery appropriately marked.
- Obtain permission and a supported baseline before publishing real customer quotations or outcome claims.

## 11. Technical SEO requirements

- Render substantive homepage copy, links and metadata as crawlable HTML.
- Use one canonical homepage URL, HTTPS and deliberate alternate-host redirects.
- Give the page a unique descriptive title and meta description.
- Use one H1 with logical subordinate headings.
- Supply matching Open Graph/social fields and an approved image.
- Include only canonical public URLs in the sitemap.
- Prevent preview/draft indexing; robots exclusion is not access control.
- Return genuine status codes for missing pages rather than a homepage with status 200.
- Make internal navigation actual crawlable links with correct destinations.
- Give content images dimensions and suitable responsive delivery; avoid layout shifts and unnecessary page weight.
- Check mobile usability, keyboard access and readable content without requiring animation or video.
- Validate the generated production HTML and live domain after release.

Structured data should describe real entities and visible information. Consider Organization and WebSite data with verified business fields. Assess SoftwareApplication markup against the actual public offer and applicable requirements. Never fabricate `aggregateRating`, reviews or missing business fields. FAQs are useful content; do not promise FAQ rich-result eligibility.

Performance goals are evaluation targets, not claimed results: aim for good real-user Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile—LCP ≤2.5 seconds, INP ≤200 milliseconds and CLS ≤0.1. Lab tests help diagnose issues but do not establish real-user performance or ranking outcomes.

Astro-specific implementation, hosting/deployment operations, CSS tokens, animation choices and layout decisions are outside this SEO/content brief.

## 12. Measurement and review

| Metric | What it answers | Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage non-brand impressions/clicks | Is the intended restaurant-POS search audience discovering the page? | Search Console page/query reporting; separate brand terms |
| Homepage CTR | Does search presentation attract relevant visits? | Search Console; interpret alongside query and position changes |
| Indexability | Can the canonical public homepage be indexed? | URL inspection, rendered HTML, canonical and sitemap checks |
| Demo CTA clicks | Which content positions prompt evaluation? | Analytics events labelled by CTA position |
| Server-accepted leads | Did enquiries actually reach the system? | Successful server response and CRM record |
| Qualified leads/completed demos | Are enquiries commercially useful? | CRM outcome tracking |
| Lead errors | Are technical failures losing enquiries? | Sanitized error categories and delivery monitoring |
| Page experience | Is the page usable and responsive? | Field data when available; documented lab checks otherwise |

Do not send names, phone numbers, emails or free-text enquiry content into analytics. A CTA click is not a lead; a lead is not a completed demo or sale.

Establish a real baseline before setting traffic and conversion targets. Review indexing after launch, then monitor page/query performance and qualified enquiries regularly. Investigate query intent and product objections before changing copy. Do not promise rankings, traffic, CAC or sales from keyword volume alone.
